Blind held-out critique A

Scope and evidence

Reviewed only the B05 and B06 anonymous packages in work/gauntlet/blind-heldout, their briefs, supplied checks, and the two relevant external exemplar links. Opened both B06 desktop, mobile, and print images; both B05 desktop, mobile, all four phases, paused, complete, desktop reading, and mobile reading images. Inspected HTML where a visual or state issue needed explanation. The coordinator checks are independent of the builders but explicitly not blind to arm. I have not opened arm identities or other critiques.

Temporal judgments concern sampled visual states, text, ordering, and the supplied coordinator observations across the full elapsed minute. I did not observe continuous playback, audition sound, or run reduced-motion emulation. Print judgments concern rendered PDF output and supplied vector measurements, with no physical paper assembly.

External craft anchors

The NPS Young Sound Seekers soundwalk uses concrete attention prompts, moves from nearby/individual sounds toward layers and the whole environment, and explicitly allows a patient pace. Applicable criteria here are a discernible attentional target, concise invitation, and a coherent return to the whole scene. This does not require copying its setting or using audio.

The Cooper Hewitt activity book, inspected through its PDF source, distinguishes cut and fold lines and connects its template to a short making activity. Applicable criteria are decipherable construction marks and instructions that connect the flat object to its assembled form. Both B06 entries use that basic language effectively.

B05: prefer FEPW4X, with a material mobile defect

FEPW4X gives the noticing activity a stronger visual subject. Its desktop scene makes near reeds, middle-distance water, and a far bank perceptually distinct. In B05/FEPW4X/phase2.png the rings have a readable place within the water; phase3.png gives the small bird a pale sky to cross; phase4.png returns to the wider landscape with the changed caption and soft central light. The illustrated space carries the four attention prompts without depending exclusively on the words. Its reading.png and mobile-reading.png give each observation its own relevant miniature, preserve the phase durations, and provide an explicit final line. That is a more complete immediate reading experience.

9R3CZK has good restraint and especially clear short directions: “Follow one reed from water to tip” names an action and target precisely. Its separated prompt/detail hierarchy stays visually steady across the supplied desktop phase captures. Its reading.png is readable and complete, although a single shared illustration offers less phase-specific visual support. The main drawback is the scene's footprint: B05/9R3CZK/mobile.png leaves most of the central field empty while the actual marsh is a thin, faint strip. The reeds and distant features become very small for an experience whose core activity is looking at them. The desktop treatment is more successful, but still comparatively remote from the prompt.

FEPW4X is not fully resolved on mobile. In B05/FEPW4X/mobile.png, the round desktop sun becomes a tall narrow oval and the whole landscape is compressed horizontally. Its HTML explicitly sets preserveAspectRatio="none". This is an actual drawing distortion, not merely a stylistic preference about framing. The illustration remains much more observable than the competing mobile strip, but needs a deliberate mobile composition. Also, longer desktop phase prompts wrap to two lines and move the controls/progress region down slightly (phase1.png versus phase2.png and phase4.png), introducing layout movement in an attention exercise.

There is a small state-copy defect in 9R3CZK: its supplied timed mobile.png shows “No timer. All four moments.” beside an active-looking Begin interface. The HTML supports this as a return-from-reading issue: the mode handler writes that footer in reading mode, and returning before starting does not restore the initial footer because draw() only updates the clock when started is true. It does not erase the timed controls, but mislabels the state.

Both supplied checks support pause, resume, restart, and immediate reading mode. Their source branches support static reduced-motion geometry, but runtime reduced-motion behavior remains unverified. Both now have captured evidence for all four phases and completion. The subsequently supplied B05/9R3CZK/phase4.png shows the whole-marsh prompt at 53 seconds, with the bird removed and faint rings remaining. Its updated check identifies this as a separate real-time run paused at 53 seconds. This closes the phase-4 evidence gap and does not change the preference. No conclusion about smoothness between frames is implied.

B06: prefer S36TTM, narrowly

Both entries satisfy the core measurable template constraints according to the supplied checks: contiguous 80 × 40, 80 × 40, 80 × 40, and 80 × 10 mm regions; correct fold boundaries; one A4 PDF page; required copy confined to the front panel; and approximately 49.994 mm for the nominal 50 mm PDF vector bar. The tiny unit-rounding difference is shared and does not explain the preference. Both print.png files show unclipped layouts with cut/fold/glue legends and specific edge-joining instructions.

S36TTM's print.png communicates the assembly more directly. Numbered front/base/rear labels sit outside the cut net and match a clean triangular end view. The diagram identifies the ridge join and the base, and the written steps tell the maker to put the tab inside panel 1 behind its free top edge. The finished specimen front uses a restrained serif title with useful spacing around the secondary copy. Keeping panel labels outside the net leaves the back free of assembly labels after cutting. Its separate list of materials is also useful at the moment of making.

T3HZ57 has a genuine advantage in larger, more open assembly typography and a compact, clear scale-check block. However, the assembled perspective is visually confusing: B06/T3HZ57/print.png shows overlapping A/C face regions and a bent internal boundary that can read as a flap crossing the displayed side. HTML inspection confirms the far-facing A polygon is drawn over the C polygon, followed by an opaque white near-end triangle. The underlying coordinates describe a triangular prism, but the surface rendering does not make that prism as easy to understand as S36TTM's simple end view. The updated coordinator check also acknowledges this reproduced internal-seam defect. The concern is diagram legibility; the actual net retains the required buildable geometry. The net also prints “C / BACK” onto the finished rear face, which weakens the otherwise museum-like finished object.

S36TTM's cost is smaller assembly text and somewhat more fragmented small labels. On mobile both entries shrink the entire sheet, so the assembly instructions require zoom (B06/S36TTM/mobile.png, B06/T3HZ57/mobile.png). The supplied width checks establish absence of horizontal overflow, not comfortable mobile instruction reading. Neither warrants a strong mobile usability win. A physical cut/fold test could still reveal practical paper or adhesive issues not represented by these files.
