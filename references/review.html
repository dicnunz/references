Inspect the finished experience

Load the part relevant to the work. These are evidence choices, not a universal checklist.

Writing and information: Read the actual content in order and at its intended length. Locate what the reader must understand or do, where context is introduced, and what each transition earns. Judge voice, rhythm, precision, and argument from passages; a typographic screenshot cannot prove them. Verify factual claims through their sources.

Print, images, objects, spaces: Inspect intended scale, distance, material, hierarchy, and relationships among parts. Show a spread or full composition as well as a revealing detail. For constructed work, check assembly, tolerances, reach, balance, and material behavior as relevant. A render establishes appearance within its assumptions; state what still requires a physical prototype.

Sound, movement, and timed media: Experience the full sequence at intended speed. Inspect entries, exits, silence, transitions, and how repetition changes. Check intelligibility and synchronization through playback. A contact sheet cannot establish timing or sound. A decode or export test establishes file validity only.

Interactive work and systems: Operate the consequential path and its relevant alternate states. Inspect response, feedback, error recovery, navigation, density, and continuity at the sizes and input methods the brief requires. An attractive resting screen cannot establish behavior or usability.

Give a concrete critique: identify the location or state, describe what it does to the audience's experience, and connect a revision to that cause. Compare with relevant source evidence without assuming reputation guarantees fitness. Independent reviewers are useful for consequential or ambiguous work when available, but never claim an independent evaluation from the maker's own opinion.
